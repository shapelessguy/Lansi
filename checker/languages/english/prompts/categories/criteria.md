# Recognising a category

How the decision model is told to recognise a word of each category of English. It does best with examples, of every form the words come in.

## Prompt: noun

A noun, in the singular or the plural: dog, dogs, child, children, house, information, mice, sheep.

## Prompt: verb

A verb, in any form: base form (go, be, get up), conjugated (goes, went, am, is, are, was, were, has, did), participle (gone, been, taken, going, being), and a modal (can, could, must, should).

## Prompt: adjective

An adjective, in any degree: big, bigger, biggest, good, better, best, beautiful, tired, interesting.

## Prompt: adverb

An adverb: today, tomorrow, now, soon, always, often, here, there, very, well, better, quickly, perhaps.

## Prompt: pronoun

A pronoun, in any case: I, me, mine, myself, you, he, him, his, she, her, hers, it, we, us, they, them, someone, something, who, whom, what, this, that.

## Prompt: article

An article or determiner: the, a, an, my, your, this, these, every, some, any, no.

## Prompt: preposition

A preposition: in, on, at, with, to, from, of, for, by, about, under, between, without.

## Prompt: conjunction

A conjunction: and, or, but, because, that, if, when, while, although, so.

## Prompt: particle

A particle: not, n't, to before an infinitive, and the up, out or off of a verb such as give up.

## Prompt: numeral

A number written as a word: one, two, three, eight, twenty, hundred, first, third.

## Prompt: interjection

A greeting or exclamation: hello, hi, bye, thanks, please, oh, wow, oops.

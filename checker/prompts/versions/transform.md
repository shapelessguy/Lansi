# Transforming a book

What a version of a book is written anew with, a part of the book at a time: the prompts the learner can start from, and change, when they make a transformed version. Each variant is one the learner is offered by its name. Every part of the book that is independent of the others, such as a story of a book of short stories, is transformed on its own.

They are not filled in by the server: they have the keywords of the prompts the learner writes, such as `{{language}}` and `{{level}}`, which the app fills in as it does in every other prompt of theirs.

## Prompt: translate

Translate the text into {{language}}. Keep its meaning, its tone and the way it is divided into paragraphs, and keep the names of people and places as they are. Write natural {{language}}, as a writer of {{language}} would have written the story, rather than a word-for-word translation.

## Prompt: level

Rewrite the text in {{language}} for a learner at level {{level}}, so that they can read the whole of it, not a summary of it. Change how it is said, never what it says.

Keep everything the text says: every event, idea, argument, example, explanation, name, number, date and quotation, in the same order. Leave nothing out and shorten nothing: a text made simpler usually needs more words than the original, not fewer, so each paragraph will often be as long as it was or longer.

Write the language a learner at {{level}} knows: common words, the grammar and the tenses of the level, sentences of a length the level can follow. Where the text uses a word or an idea too hard for the level, such as a technical term, keep it and explain it in simple words, rather than leave it out. Keep the sentences joined as connected, natural prose, with the linking words of the level that say how one thing follows from another, gives a reason, a contrast or an example; never cut the text into a list of short, separate statements. Keep the tone and the voice of the author: a story stays a story, and an argument stays an argument.

Keep each paragraph a paragraph of its own, and a list a list.
